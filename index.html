How it looks

Your picture gets bent 69 ways: colour channels pulled apart, saturation pushed until it clips, contrast stretched, the fine grain isolated from the shapes, single bits of each pixel pulled out on their own.

Every version goes through a Fourier transform, which turns anything that repeats into a bright dot. A dot that stands alone, away from the streaks edges make and the fuzz textures make, is a candidate.

How it decides

A candidate has to clear a prominence of 20.77. That number was frozen before anything unknown was scanned: 1.25 times the strongest dot found in 24 pictures known to be clean (camera photos, noise, plain renders, JPEG'd graphics).

Then it has to recur: the same spacing and angle in at least two different views AND at least two separate quarters of the picture. That's PATTERN. One view or one quarter only is WEAK. Nothing over the line: no strong pattern.

A grid sitting exactly on the JPEG 8 / 16 pixel block lattice gets its own word, LATTICE (threshold 23.84), and never counts as PATTERN.

What it can't see

  • Stripes lined up exactly with the picture's edges. That's on purpose, so JPEG blocks and resizing never cry wolf.
  • A mark that changes with every picture, or isn't periodic at all. One picture can only show a pattern that repeats inside it.
  • A watermark like SynthID doesn't repeat inside one picture. It lines up ACROSS pictures. That's what Many images tests: it found Google's mark in their images, and it's blind to marks hidden on the 8 / 16 px grid or keyed per picture.

A found pattern is a fingerprint of how a picture was made (a camera, an upscaler, a decoder). It's not proof of hidden data.
