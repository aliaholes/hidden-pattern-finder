Fingerprint

What am I looking at?

  • A fingerprint is the average spectrum of many pictures' fine noise (each picture minus a blurred copy). Content averages out; whatever the camera or generator adds the same way every time stays. Upsamplers and image decoders often leave a lattice of dots, as Corvi et al. (2023) showed for diffusion models.
  • How it's computed: green channel, centre 512 x 512 crop (offset aligned to 8 px, no resizing), residual = crop minus a Gaussian blur (sigma 1.5 px, applied exactly in the frequency domain), 2-D FFT, power averaged over the set, shown as log power. The bright cross through the centre is the picture edges and normal horizontal/vertical structure, not a mark.
  • Confounds. Content (the same scene or style in every picture shows up too), format (JPEG adds an 8 px grid, PNG doesn't), resizing (moves or erases peaks), and re-encoding: social sites recompress and resize, which usually wipes these traces. Compare like with like: same format, size and kind of content.
  • Suggestive, not proof. A different-looking fingerprint means the pictures were made differently. It doesn't say by what, and it isn't a watermark reading.

What the file says

Read on this device from the file's own bytes: C2PA / Content Credentials, XMP, PNG text, EXIF. Signatures are not checked, and metadata is easy to strip or fake: if it's missing, that means nothing.

    Open watermark reader (DWT-DCT)

      The pattern, made visible

      How it looks

      Your picture gets bent 69 ways: colour channels pulled apart, saturation pushed until it clips, contrast stretched, the fine grain isolated from the shapes, single bits of each pixel pulled out on their own.

      Every version goes through a Fourier transform, which turns anything that repeats into a bright dot. A dot that stands alone, away from the streaks edges make and the fuzz textures make, is a candidate.

      How it decides

      A candidate has to clear a prominence of 20.77. That number was frozen before anything unknown was scanned: 1.25 times the strongest dot found in 24 pictures known to be clean (camera photos, noise, plain renders, JPEG'd graphics).

      Then it has to recur: the same spacing and angle in at least two different views AND at least two separate quarters of the picture. That's PATTERN. One view or one quarter only is WEAK. Nothing over the line: no strong pattern.

      A grid sitting exactly on the JPEG 8 / 16 pixel block lattice gets its own word, LATTICE (threshold 23.84), and never counts as PATTERN.

      What it can't see

      • Stripes lined up exactly with the picture's edges. That's on purpose, so JPEG blocks and resizing never cry wolf.
      • A mark that changes with every picture, or isn't periodic at all. One picture can only show a pattern that repeats inside it.
      • A watermark like SynthID doesn't repeat inside one picture. It lines up ACROSS pictures. That's what Many images tests. It can't see marks keyed to each picture or adapted to the content, and on the 8 / 16 px grid it only counts what passes a much stricter test.
      • Lining up is not proof of a watermark. Anything fixed in image coordinates lines up too: a decoder's or upscaler's fingerprint, a border, a vignette. Use a control set from another source to rule those out.

      A found pattern is a fingerprint of how a picture was made (a camera, an upscaler, a decoder). It's not proof of hidden data.
