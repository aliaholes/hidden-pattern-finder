# How Hive detects and attributes AI images, and what we can borrow

Research notes from Oct 17, 2026. Nothing here is pushed. **[Hive]** means Hive says it; **[Inferred]** means our reading; **[Indep.]** means an independent source.

## 1. What Hive says (primary sources)
- **[Hive] One endpoint, two heads.** One head is binary (`ai_generated` / `not_ai_generated`). The other is a softmax over ~100 source classes: midjourney, dalle, stablediffusion, stablediffusionxl, flux, flux2, imagen, imagen4, gemini, gemini3, 4o, gptimage1_5, grok, ideogram, adobefirefly, gan, …, plus `other_image_generators`, `inconclusive` and `none`. Scores within each head sum to 1. A separate deepfake (face-swap) model runs alongside. https://docs.thehive.ai/docs/ai-image-and-video-detection , https://docs.thehive.ai/reference/ai-generated-image-and-video-detection-1
- **[Hive] C2PA is a separate field.** When present, C2PA metadata is returned on its own, with the caveat that it "can be stripped or falsified". The classifier does not depend on it. (Same docs.)
- **[Hive] Also sold as an NVIDIA NIM.** It is described as "optimized for … media generated by popular AI generative engines". https://docs.api.nvidia.com/nim/reference/hive-ai-generated-image-detection
- **[Hive] Patent US 12,321,831 B1** (Castle Global, filed 2023-07-25, granted 2025-06-03; inventors Karpman, Weber, Guo). It claims pre-trained deep-learning classifiers per modality that output confidence scores "based on parameters learned … during a training stage". For video it classifies frames, and it covers whole or partial diffusion edits. Nothing in it specifies hand-crafted frequency features. https://exa.ai/library/legal/patent/ys1fg8kfwmd (see also Google Patents US12321831B1)
- **[Hive] Related patent US 12,217,480:** learned visual-embedding similarity trained on altered pairs (aimed at robustness to edits). https://exa.ai/library/legal/patent/bp78dlqf8qx
- **[Hive] Blog** on the UChicago study ("clear winner"). It credits the result to its large, diverse generator training data. https://thehive.ai/blog/clear-winner-study-shows-hives-ai-generated-image-detection-api-is-best-in-class

## 2. Independent evaluations
| Study | Result for Hive | Failure modes |
|---|---|---|
| Ha & Passananti et al. 2024 (UChicago, art; arXiv 2402.03214) | 98.03% accuracy, 0% false positives, 3.17% false negatives | Weak on AI images processed with Glaze; also tested against JPEG, noise and upscaling perturbations |
| PeerJ 2025, western blots (doi:10.7717/peerj.18988), n=48+48 | Sensitivity 0.19, specificity 0.875, AUC 0.51 (chance) | Out-of-domain scientific images; cropping pushed false negatives to 46/48 |
| CBC News 2025 (3 images, run twice) | 3/3 correct, including the compressed X re-post | Tiny sample |
| Adversarial robustness, arXiv 2410.01574 | Commercial detectors, Hive among them, can be evaded by attacks | Adversarial perturbations |
| IJARCS 2026 (doi:10.26483/ijarcs.v17i3.7514), deepfakes | Accuracy drops on Celeb-DF and real-world samples | Confident wrong answers |

Takeaway: excellent on in-distribution generator output (art, photoreal), poor out of domain, and vulnerable to adversarial attacks. No independent study we found evaluates the **source-attribution** head.

## 3. The science, and what Hive most likely does
- **Fingerprints exist.** Marra et al. 2019 (GAN fingerprints from averaged noise residuals) and Yu et al. 2019 (learned per-GAN fingerprints) showed that each generator leaves a characteristic trace, so per-model attribution is possible.
- **Wang et al. 2020, CNNDetection:** a ResNet-50 trained on one GAN with blur and JPEG augmentation generalizes surprisingly well.
- **Corvi et al. 2023 (ICASSP):** diffusion models show periodic spectral peaks in averaged residual spectra, from upsampling and VAE decoders (code is Apache-2.0: github.com/grip-unina/DMimageDetection). The peaks fade under compression and resizing.
- **Ojha et al. 2023, UnivFD:** a linear probe on frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 features generalizes to unseen generators.
- **DIRE (Wang et al. 2023):** diffusion reconstruction error. It is costly and later shown to be partly a JPEG/format shortcut.
- **Datasets:** GenImage (Zhu et al. 2023, 8 generators, ~1.3M images), Synthbuster (Bammey 2023, RAISE photos re-created by 9 diffusion models; a strong held-out test), and Community Forensics (Park & Owens, CVPR 2025: 2.7M images from 4,803 generators; shows that generator diversity is what drives generalization).
- **[Inferred, moderate–high confidence]** Hive is most likely a large supervised deep classifier: a CNN or ViT backbone, probably with pre-trained semantic features à la UnivFD, plus a multi-class source head. It would be trained on a very large, continuously refreshed corpus sampled from each generator, with heavy augmentation (JPEG, resizing, crops, screenshots). That is the Community Forensics recipe at commercial scale. Evidence:
  - the patent's "pre-trained classifier/learned parameters" language
  - a source taxonomy that adds new models within weeks of release (it already lists gemini3, flux2 and gptimage1_5), which only makes sense if the data pipeline is retrained per model
  - robustness to re-compression in the CBC test
  - Hive's own credit to its "rich collection of generative AI datasets"
- **[Inferred]** Spectral and upsampling artifacts are probably among the cues the network learns, but there is no evidence of an explicit Fourier-feature pipeline. Watermarks are not required (C2PA is reported separately).

## 4. Feasibility for hidden-pattern-finder
**(a) Spectral fingerprint view: easy, about 1 day.** We already compute high-pass residuals and FFTs. Add a "Fingerprint" tab that averages residual power spectra across a dropped set (log scale, optional radial whitening) and shows it next to the control set's spectrum. This is a display only, a forensic aid and not a verdict. It is reliable on PNG and native-size files and degrades under JPEG or resizing (Corvi).

**(b) On-device classifier: feasible, about 3–5 days plus evaluation.**
| Candidate | License | Size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community Forensics `OwensLab/commfor-model-224` / `-384` | MIT (weights); dataset CC-BY-4.0 research | ViT-S class, ~22M params, ~90 MB fp32, ~25 MB int8 | Best fit: permissive, modern, trained on 4.8k generators. **Binary real/fake, not attribution.** |
| Corvi DMimageDetection (ResNet-50 variants) | Apache-2.0 | ~95 MB | Binary; trained on Latent Diffusion; robust-ish to JPEG |
| UnivFD (CLIP ViT-L/14 + linear) | Check repo; CLIP weights are MIT | ~1.2 GB | Too large for the browser; a ViT-B/32 variant is ~350 MB |
| CNNDetection (Wang 2020) | CC BY-NC-SA | ~95 MB | Non-commercial; GAN-era |

Accuracy: published numbers are in each paper (e.g. Community Forensics reports averages around the mid-90s% on its comprehensive eval). **Do not quote these as our numbers; measure them.**

- **Attribution** needs our own small head: freeze the CommFor or CLIP backbone in Python, train a logistic or linear head on per-generator embeddings (GenImage classes plus Synthbuster plus our NB sets), and export the backbone and head to ONNX for onnxruntime-web (WASM/WebGPU). That's about 1 extra week. Add an "unknown" option via a confidence threshold.
- Everything stays offline: the model is shipped as a static file and inference runs in the worker.

**Plan**
1. Fingerprint view (a), with the harness running it on folders.
2. Binary CommFor-224 ONNX (int8), with labels phrased as a probability and not a verdict.
3. Evaluation script with **held-out generators** (train the head on GenImage; test on Synthbuster and on Banana100/NB data never seen in training). Report per-source AUC, TPR at 1% FPR, and a confusion matrix, each for PNG, JPEG q75 and resized ×0.5. Also report false positives on our Commons camera set.
4. Only then the attribution head, with an "unknown" class and a disclaimer.

## 5. Quick experiment (local data)
`tools/harness/fingerprints.py` computes green-channel residuals (image minus Gaussian blur, sigma 1.5) on 512² centre crops and averages their power spectra per source. Output: `shots/fingerprints.png` (local, not committed).

| Panel | N | Look |
|---|---|---|
| Camera, Commons JPEG | 149 | Smooth radial falloff plus axis lines (edges), faint 8-px JPEG grid points |
| Camera, photos set | 161 | Same as Commons |
| NB Pro step 1 (Banana100 PNG) | 21 | **Distinct:** periodic dotted peaks along both axes (grid-like), typical of upsampling/decoder artifacts |
| NB Pro, 200 random chain steps | 200 | **Very distinct:** strong four-lobe mid-frequency ring from accumulated iterative re-generation |
| NB2 via X (JPEG q85) | 59 | Diffuse; no clear peaks. X re-encoding seems to wash them out |
| Photo + SD1 dwtDct mark | 160 | Indistinguishable from camera at this view |

Conclusion: native Nano Banana Pro PNGs look visibly different from camera photos, and the edit chains are extremely different. Re-encoded X copies do not. Confounds:
- content (only 10–11 Banana100 seeds; the chains are highly correlated)
- format (PNG vs camera JPEG)
- resolution and crop position
- N=21 for step 1

So this is suggestive and not a validated fingerprint.
